1. About This Policy

Samsung TV Remote is a utility that discovers and controls compatible Samsung TVs over the user's local network. This policy explains what the app stores on your device, what it sends to Google and Firebase services, and the choices available to you.

You do not need to create a personal account to discover, pair with, or control a TV.

Samsung TV Remote is independent from Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. “Samsung” and related marks belong to their respective owners.

2. Information We Collect

Information stored locally on your device

To make the remote work across sessions, the app stores information in its Android app sandbox, including:

  • TV name, brand, local IP address, port, protocol, connection settings, discovery aliases or identifiers, and MAC address when available or entered for Wake-on-LAN;
  • Samsung pairing or session credentials needed to reconnect to a TV;
  • room labels, favorites, last-used and last-seen times, selected TV apps or quick-launch items, and other device preferences; and
  • language, theme, navigation, notification, onboarding, consent, and other app settings.

These TV records and remote commands are used for local control and are not intentionally uploaded to BlueSpace Studios. Some TV information, such as installed-app names or icons, may be read from the TV and kept temporarily on the device for display.

Information you provide

  • Manual setup: any TV name, IP address, port, MAC address, or other connection value that you enter.
  • Email feedback: if you choose the email option, the app opens your email client with a draft containing the phone model, app version, Android version, and a blank area for your feedback. Nothing is emailed by the app until you choose an email provider and send it.

Analytics and technical information

Firebase Analytics receives app events such as discovery, connection, pairing, command, update, notification-permission, rating, and error outcomes. Events may include app version, device manufacturer and model, Android version, locale, time zone or country, low-cardinality connection labels, and coarse network status such as Wi-Fi, VPN, cellular, or whether a LAN scan is possible.

Analytics and other Firebase SDKs may use identifiers such as a Firebase app-instance ID, Firebase Installation ID, Crashlytics installation identifier, and Android Advertising ID where available. The app is designed not to put TV IP addresses, MAC addresses, pairing PINs, authentication tokens, or raw WebSocket data in Analytics events.

These analytics events are sent through Google's Firebase SDKs. BlueSpace Studios does not copy them into a separate analytics server or database; we may only view the reports and events that Google makes available in the Firebase console. Google controls the provider-side collection, processing, retention, and console visibility.

Crash and stability information

Firebase Crashlytics may receive crash stack traces, non-fatal error reports, device and app metadata, connection phase, TV brand and protocol, port, breadcrumbs, and developer-defined diagnostics. The current app may also attach the display name you assigned to a TV to a crash report. The app is designed to avoid logging credentials, PINs, IP addresses, MAC addresses, and raw TV messages, but no diagnostic system can guarantee that every third-party exception detail is free of sensitive text.

3. Permissions and Device Access

The app requests or uses the following Android capabilities:

  • Internet, network state, Wi-Fi state, and multicast access to discover and control TVs on your local network and to communicate with Google/Firebase services;
  • a connected-device foreground service and Wi-Fi wake lock to keep an active TV session working when the app is minimized;
  • notifications, when you allow them, for connection status and remote-control actions;
  • boot-completed access to restore the app's quick-control state after a restart; and
  • gyroscope and gravity sensors, when available, for the optional air-mouse feature. Sensor readings are used on the device to create cursor movement and are not intentionally uploaded.

The current Android build does not request contacts, camera, phone, SMS, or precise-location permissions. The app does not request a microphone permission itself. If you tap voice input, Android launches the speech-recognition service selected on your device; that service may use the microphone and may send audio to its own provider. Samsung TV Remote receives the resulting text and lets you review or edit it before sending it to the TV.

6. Advertising and Consent

The current build serves Google Mobile Ads banner, interstitial, and app-open ads on eligible screens. Full-screen ads are blocked during onboarding and text-entry flows; banner availability follows the screen's ad eligibility. The Google User Messaging Platform is used to request and record the consent choices required in applicable regions before ad requests are enabled.

For users in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland, Google UMP may show a consent form before ads are requested when the applicable rules require it. You can choose whether to allow personalized advertising or decline or limit consent. Depending on Google's response and your choice, ad requests may be limited to non-personalized ads or not made. When Google marks privacy options as required, you can withdraw or change your choice from Settings → Privacy Options.

Depending on your location, settings, and consent, Google may serve personalized or non-personalized advertising. Google may process the Android Advertising ID, IP address, approximate location, device and app information, ad-request context, and ad interactions. Resetting or deleting the Advertising ID in Android settings may reduce personalized advertising, but does not stop all ad requests.

When Google provides a privacy-options control, you can revisit ad choices from the app's Settings → Privacy Options screen. If the control is not shown, the applicable region or Google consent status may not require it. We do not directly sell your saved-TV records or email feedback. Advertising providers may process identifiers and technical data under their own terms, and applicable law may treat some personalized-ad processing as “sharing.”

8. Retention and Deletion

You do not create an account for the TV-control features, and BlueSpace Studios does not maintain a searchable copy of your saved-TV records or Firebase Analytics events.

  • Local TV records, credentials, and preferences: kept on your device until you remove the TV, clear app data, or uninstall the app. The current Android backup rules exclude the app database and its settings and pairing-credential files from cloud backup and device transfer.
  • Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics: sent to Google only when the relevant SDK records an event or diagnostic. The Android app does not set a 30–40-minute deletion timer and does not keep a second copy. Google and Firebase control provider-side retention and Firebase console visibility under their project settings and current service practices.
  • Advertising and Remote Config: retained by Google or the relevant provider according to its settings, service terms, and legal obligations.
  • Email feedback: if you choose to send the draft email, the message is stored by your selected email provider and mailbox rather than by an in-app account database.

Temporary visibility or disappearance of an event in the Firebase console is not a promise of a specific retention period. We may retain email information or other information we directly receive when needed for legal claims, security investigations, fraud prevention, or legal compliance.

12. Security

Local records are stored inside Android's app sandbox. Pairing/session credentials are stored locally and are not intentionally logged. Firebase, Google Mobile Ads, and Remote Config communications use provider-supported HTTPS/TLS connections; Google and Firebase describe additional encryption and access controls in their own documentation.

TV communication takes place on your local network. Some compatible TV protocols use unencrypted local HTTP or WebSocket traffic, so use the app only on a network you trust. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.
